# Modes

Which of four modes a doc is, and what each mode holds. Read it when you start a doc, split one, or review a doc that
does two jobs.

## One doc, one mode

Give each doc one mode. When a doc needs a second mode, split it into two docs and link them. A reader in one mode
skips the text of the other mode, and misses what that text says.

| the reader wants to | mode | name the doc by |
|---|---|---|
| learn by doing, new to the subject | tutorial | what the learner builds |
| reach a goal they know | how-to | the task |
| look up a fact | reference | the thing it describes |
| understand why | explanation | the topic |

## The rules of each mode

- **Tutorial.** Say first what the learner builds. Make each step give a result they can see, and say what they
  see. Give an explanation one clause and a link, because the learner is here to do, not to read.
- **How-to.** Solve the reader's task, and assume they know the basics. Write actions only. Give a fork where the
  task branches, with its condition first.
- **Reference.** Describe only, and be complete and exact. Mirror the structure of the thing described, so the reader
  finds an entry where they expect it.
- **Explanation.** Keep to one bounded topic. Give the context: the decisions, the constraints and the options that
  were rejected. An opinion goes here, and in no other mode.

## Review

Walk each doc that the change adds or edits. Each line is a blocker unless marked (warning).

- A doc that holds two modes, such as steps inside a reference, or a why inside a how-to longer than one clause
  beside its step.
- A tutorial step with no result the learner can see.
- A how-to that teaches the basics, or a reference that gives an opinion.
- A reference whose order does not follow the thing it describes.
- A doc named by its mode ("Guide") and not by its task or topic.
